An Interview with the Lemon Twigs - 10/6/2023

Ed: So Bobby, where are you From?
Justin Bobby: I’m from Hicksville, New York.
Mr. Mistake: Hicksville, California.
JB: Hicksville. It’s not hicky-- I Promise.
ED: I heard it’s great this time of year.
JB: It’s great.“Land of the Joels”.
ED: Which ones?
JB: Do you know Billy? Not Billy Tibbals... We don’t talk about Billy Tibbals in Hicksville.It’s Billy Joels. We went to high school with him. We went to high school together.
ED: So Bobby-- like Billy? Are you named after one of the several dozen Billy’s [in Hicksville]?
JB: Well, I actually don’t know anybody personally named Billy. I do not have any acquaintances or friends named Billy. That is only outside people.
(to SV) I think its the same for you, you dont know anyone named Billy.
Sill Vann: Uhhhhhmmmmm...............
JB: That you personally talk to every day?
SV: That I “talk to every day”?? JB: There are no Billies in your life.
SV: Not at a constant. Not at a constant in my life necessarily. Yeah, not really. Some they come, and I end up seeing one every once and a while.
ED: Do you believe in any conspiracy theories?
SV: I guess I do...
JB: I believe that Elvis is still alive.
SV: Yeah, yeah, yeah! He believes that Elvis is still alive.
ED: Is this true?
JB: I think so, because there’s no proof of him being dead. Where is his headstone?? You wanna show me the one where his name is spelled wrong? Then, show it to me.
ED: See thats the thing, I don’t think thats crazy but where are they keeping them? That’s my thing; where is Michael Jackson? Where is Elvis?? In the disney vault???
SV: They’re just in Hawaii making more movies, ya know? That’s where Elvis made some of his best movies-- well, his worst movies. Highest paying.
JB: How did you get to Hawaii in the fifties? By boat?
ED: There’s a train tracks train. With the whole global thing and the icebergs... It’s all rust now. But you could take the train.
SV: There’s an underground train that goes from here to Hawaii--
ED: Underwater.
SV: --that you used to take back in the old days, before they had planes.
ED: What do you do in your free time?
MM: I watch T.V. and I play guitar, and I record songs. And I play drums as well. And I write lyrics and eat food. And drive cars.
ED: Are you a writer or a performer?
MM: I am both, and I like doing both. A lot.
ED: Are you a writer or are you a performer?
SV: I guess I’m both, technically.
MM: You are obviously both.
SV: Technically. But my heart is more in the writing of the thing. But I like to perform, too.
ED: You guys put on one hell of a show.
MM: Thank you very much
SV: Thank you.
ED: Truly.
SV: Thanks!
ED: Where is your favorite place to play?
SV: Well, Milwaukee is pretty cool. We’ve been having some-
MM: Where?
SV: Milwaukee IS pretty cool.
MM: Oh yeah
ED: Where is that?
SV: Its right uhm.... I don’t know where the coordinates are, but I think it’s... If you put my location, you would find it. On your phone.
ED: Last question- who’s your favorite member of the Traveling Willberries?
SV: Lucky. Lucky Wilson.
ED: I like Roy Orbison.
SV: He has that voice, he has the voice. THE voice. Of the Willberries.
ED: You know he died, right after their first album came out (1985).
SV: And then he turned into a ROCKING CHAIR! It was horrible.
ED: How do you mean?
SV: Well, ya know, He turned into a rocking chair. After he passed away, they were performing with him. One day he was Roy Orbison, and the next day he was a rocking chair. Look it up. Look it up. You will find it’s true.
ED: Did you ever see it at The MET?
SV: Like The Metropolitan Museum?
ED: Yeah, It’s a traveling exhibition. I’m not sure where it is now.
SV: Did I see Roy Orbison there? Oh, no.
ED: Well, the chair.
SV: Oh, the chair. Maybe not that chair. Maybe one of his friends.
ED: Heard it’s nice there this time of year.
ED: Well listen, thanks guys! That’ll do it.
JB, MM & SV: *harmonizing in unison* No Problem!
FIN.

THE WITCH by Andrea the Braden

Summertime is the best time for witches. Since the invention of sunscreen, they have been venturing into the streets. The cold blooded creatures are partial to the warm weather.

It was autumn now, and the shop was cold. The old wooden windows let a draft that covered the tools in frost, making them harder to use. It took longer to cut up the bodies.

This never occurred to her, for she was oblivious to most things. The cauldron needed stirring, so she stirred it. Wielding the large pipe in her gloved hands, she rowed slow, even circles into the orange liquid. It’s viscosity pulled her, moving her body slowly from her arms, up into her shoulders, through her torso and her hips. “Cleaver, Saw, Scissors, Knife.” She spoke to nobody, monotone syllables echoing into unresponsive ears of the cadaver. Pools of blood gathered on the table, glimmering as they dripped onto the exposed portions of her skin. She stuck the bodies together and heaved them over to the pot, releasing them gracelessly over the cauldron.

Leatherman would be pleased...
